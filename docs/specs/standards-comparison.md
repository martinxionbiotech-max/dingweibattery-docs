# Battery Standards Comparison

Battery standards are not interchangeable. They define different test temperatures, group-size conventions, and terminal layouts. Understanding these differences is essential for correct specification and comparison.

## CCA Test Basis Comparison

CCA is measured at different temperatures depending on the standard, which is why the same battery can show different CCA values under different standards.

| Standard | Test Temperature | Cut-off Basis | Market |
|---|---|---|---|
| JIS | −15°C | JIS-defined | Japan, Asia |
| EN | −18°C | EN-defined | Europe |
| SAE | −18°C | SAE-defined | North America |
| DIN | −18°C | DIN-defined | Germany, Europe |

## Group Size Differences

- **JIS (N-series)** — N150, N200, etc., size classes for Japanese/Asian vehicles
- **DIN** — DIN88, DIN100, etc., European size classes
- **BCI Group Sizes** — numeric group sizes (e.g., 24, 27, 31, 49) for North America

## Terminal Conventions

- **JIS** — typically smaller posts, often with distinct polarity conventions
- **DIN** — recessed terminals, standard European layout
- **BCI/SAE** — top posts or side terminals depending on group

## Why This Matters

Comparing a JIS CCA to an EN CCA is an apples-to-oranges comparison. The temperature difference alone shifts the number. When specifying a battery, always:

1. Confirm the standard the number refers to
2. Compare only within the same standard
3. Confirm terminal layout and polarity
4. Confirm the group size fits your vehicle
