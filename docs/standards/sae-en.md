# SAE & EN Standards

SAE (Society of Automotive Engineers) and EN (European Norm) standards govern automotive batteries in North America and Europe respectively. Both test CCA at −18°C, but differ in group sizing and other conventions.

## SAE Standard (North America)

- **CCA test temperature**: −18°C
- **Group sizing**: Uses BCI group sizes (e.g., Group 24, 27, 31, 49)
- **Terminals**: Top posts or side terminals depending on group
- **Market**: North America

## EN Standard (Europe)

- **CCA test temperature**: −18°C
- **Group sizing**: Uses European size classes (e.g., DIN88, DIN100, or EN equivalents)
- **Terminals**: Recessed DIN-style terminals
- **Market**: Europe

## Key Similarities and Differences

- **Temperature**: Both SAE and EN test at −18°C (unlike JIS at −15°C)
- **Cranking current**: Both define a cold cranking current at −18°C
- **Group sizing**: Different conventions (BCI vs DIN/EN)
- **Terminals**: Different layouts

## Practical Implication

Although SAE and EN both test at −18°C, their group sizing and terminal conventions differ, so a battery is rarely interchangeable between North American and European vehicles without confirmation. Always confirm:

1. Group size fits the vehicle
2. Terminal layout and polarity match
3. CCA meets the vehicle's cranking requirement
