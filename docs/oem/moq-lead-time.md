# MOQ & Lead Time

Minimum order quantity (MOQ) and lead time determine how you plan OEM and private-label battery sourcing. Understanding both helps you forecast inventory and cash flow.

## Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ)

MOQ is the smallest production run a manufacturer will accept for a given model or specification.

- **Standard models** typically have lower MOQs (mixing existing models is usually permitted)
- **Custom specifications** may require higher MOQs (new tooling, custom case design, or specialized components)
- MOQ may be quoted per model, per container, or per production run

## Lead Time Components

Lead time is the total time from confirmed order to delivery. It typically includes:

1. **Pre-production** — specification confirmation, sampling, and approval
2. **Production** — the manufacturing run itself
3. **Quality & documentation** — batch testing and document preparation
4. **Shipping** — ocean freight, customs, and final delivery

## Planning Guidance

- Confirm MOQ and lead time **before** committing to a specification
- Account for sample approval cycles in your timeline
- Build buffer for shipping variability and customs clearance
- Request batch documentation lead time explicitly

## How to Confirm

MOQ and lead time vary by model, specification complexity, and current production schedule. Contact the manufacturer with your target volumes, models, and destination to receive a firm quote. See [RFQ Guide](rfq-guide.md) to prepare a complete request.
